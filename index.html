About

I'm a Bioengineering major at UC Berkeley (graduating May 2027) with minors in Electrical Engineering and Computer Science. My work sits at the intersection of neural signal processing, clinical neuroscience, and medical technology.

I conduct research at UCSF in the Coma Neuroscience Lab under Dr. Eddy Amorim, analyzing intracranial ECoG/EEG recordings from ICU patients to study coma recovery. I'm also working with the Larson Advanced Imaging Group on novel pharmacokinetic models for hyperpolarized 13C-pyruvate cardiac MRI.

Previously, I was a research intern at the Gonzalez Lab at UCSF Neurology, studying information transfer in neural circuits controlling learned behavior. I'm interested in graduate programs in computational neuroscience, medical imaging, and BME.

Used COMSOL Multiphysics in BIOE 102 (Biomechanics) to simulate structural and mechanical behavior in clinically relevant biomedical scenarios. Built and solved finite element models working through geometry construction, material property assignment, boundary condition setup, meshing, and post-processing.

Lab 1: Hip Prosthesis Stress Analysis

Modeled a Co-Cr-Mo alloy hip replacement stem and femoral head under physiological loading. Imported CAD geometry, assigned degraded bone stiffness boundary conditions to simulate a revision-surgery scenario, and computed von Mises stress and first principal stress distributions.
